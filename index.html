Builder setup: switch on email for everyone (one time, about 5 minutes)

Only the person publishing this app does this, once. It connects a free EmailJS account to a Gmail address that sends the alerts. Afterwards, everyone who uses your copy just types their email.

  1. Sign up free at emailjs.com.
  2. Email Services → Add New Service → Gmail → Connect Account. On Google's permission screen, tick "Send email on your behalf", then click Create Service. Copy the Service ID (looks like service_ab12cd3).
  3. Email Templates → Create New Template. Set:
    • Subject: {{subject}}
    • Content: {{message}}
    • To Email (right-hand side): {{to_email}}. Don't skip this one, or every alert goes to you instead of the user.
    • From Name: Fallen Angel Monitor
    Click Save, then copy the Template ID (looks like template_xy98z7).
  4. Account → General: copy the Public Key.
  5. Paste all three below, enter your email in Your email above, and click Send test. When it says Sent, click Download configured app.
  6. Use the downloaded file from then on: open it, send it to classmates, or host it. Email works in it for everyone.

The Public Key is designed to be visible in web pages, so it's safe to build into the file. EmailJS's free plan covers a few hundred emails a month, which is plenty for a class project.

Plug in Bloomberg data

A web page can't connect directly to the Bloomberg API: it needs a licensed Terminal session. Instead, export ratings from the Bloomberg Excel Add-in on any Terminal (for example in your school's Bloomberg lab), and this app compares each new snapshot against the previous ones to flag IG to HY crossings.

  1. In Excel on a Terminal, list securities in column A (for example F US Equity, or a bond CUSIP followed by Corp).
  2. Add the columns =BDP(A2,"NAME"), =BDP(A2,"RTG_SP"), =BDP(A2,"RTG_MOODY"), =BDP(A2,"RTG_FITCH"), and optionally =BDP(A2,"INDUSTRY_SECTOR").
  3. Save as CSV with the headers Ticker,Name,RTG_SP,RTG_MOODY,RTG_FITCH,INDUSTRY_SECTOR. Add a Date column to load several historical snapshots at once (rating history is on the Terminal's CRPR <GO> screen).
  4. Import the file below. Re-import later, or use Watch file (Chrome/Edge) so every check re-reads the file your sheet saves.

What counts as a fallen angel

A fallen angel is an issuer rated investment grade that is downgraded into high yield ("junk"). The line sits between BBB- and BB+ on the S&P and Fitch scales, and between Baa3 and Ba1 on Moody's.

These events matter because many insurance, pension and IG-index mandates can't hold high-yield debt. Downgrades bring forced selling, wider spreads and an index migration (once the index rating crosses the line, the bonds leave IG indices at month-end). Researchers have documented price overshoot and later recovery, which is why fallen-angel ETFs exist.

How detection works

  1. Crawl: every few minutes the app queries the GDELT global news index and Google News for downgrade language ("cut to junk", "below investment grade", "speculative grade", rating codes like BB+/Ba1).
  2. Classify: each headline is parsed for the rating agency, the issuer, the old and new ratings, and the direction. Upgrades, affirmations and moves deeper within junk are discarded.
    • Confirmed the headline shows an IG to HY move ("cut to junk", "Baa3 to Ba1", or a downgrade to BB+/Ba1, the top HY notch, which can only come from IG).
    • To verify a downgrade to an HY rating where the prior rating isn't stated.
    • Watch the issuer is at risk: negative watch, review or outlook, or "could be cut to junk".
  3. Deduplicate: stories about the same issuer and agency within three weeks are merged into one alert, with every source listed.
  4. Alert: new confirmed events are emailed to you with the issuer, agency, rating move, a company summary and research links.
  5. Bloomberg snapshots (optional) are compared one at a time, so a change in RTG_SP, RTG_MOODY or RTG_FITCH across the line raises an alert straight from Terminal data.

Limitations

  • Alerts run while this page is open (a pinned tab works). Your settings and alert history are saved in this browser.
  • News parsing is heuristic, so always confirm on the rating agency's site before acting. The links on each alert make that one click.
  • Agencies publish full rating actions behind free or paid logins. This app links to them but doesn't scrape them.
